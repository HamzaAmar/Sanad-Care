# CONTEXT ENGINEERING: Family Care Page

## Page Purpose & Goals
Create a secondary informational page (NOT homepage style) for families in Marrakech struggling to care for elderly, post-operative, or chronically ill loved ones at home. This page exists in the main navigation to:
- Connect emotionally with overwhelmed family caregivers
- Show how professional home nursing removes their burden
- Build trust with local Moroccan families
- Drive conversions (consultation bookings, relief inquiries)
- Rank for family care + home nursing Marrakech keywords

## Target Audience Profile
PRIMARY: Family members (25-65 years old) in Marrakech who are:
- Caring for elderly parents/grandparents at home
- Supporting a family member recovering from surgery
- Managing a loved one with chronic illness (diabetes, heart disease, stroke recovery, cancer)
- Balancing work/children with caregiving responsibilities
- Feeling exhausted, guilty, or overwhelmed

SECONDARY: Adult children living abroad worried about parents in Morocco

Geographic focus: Marrakech, Morocco (MVP scope)
Language: English (with cultural understanding of Moroccan family dynamics)
Audience: Moroccans AND medical tourists with family in Morocco

## Emotional Journey & Storytelling Arc

### BEGINNING (Section 1): The Struggle
**Emotion:** Stress, exhaustion, guilt, overwhelm
**Show the reality:** What families face daily

### MIDDLE (Sections 2-5): The Transformation
**Emotion:** Hope, relief, understanding
**Show the solution:** How we remove each burden piece by piece

### END (Section 6): The Freedom
**Emotion:** Peace, gratitude, joy, family connection restored
**Show the outcome:** Families being families again, not just caregivers

## Content Structure & Storytelling Flow

### Section 1: The Hidden Weight Families Carry (Problem Statement)
**Tone:** Deeply empathetic & validating
**Goal:** Make families feel SEEN and understood
**Paint the picture of daily struggles:**
- Waking up every 2 hours to give medication

## Writing Style Guidelines

**DO:**
- ✅ Write like you're talking to your exhausted sister at 2 AM
- ✅ Use second person ("You're tired. You're scared. We see you.")
- ✅ Name specific scenarios families recognize instantly
- ✅ Validate feelings (guilt, frustration, fear, exhaustion)
- ✅ Give permission to ask for help
- ✅ Show transformation (before → after)
- ✅ Be vulnerable and human
- ✅ Use short paragraphs (2-3 sentences max)

**DON'T:**
- ❌ Use corporate/clinical language ("We provide comprehensive solutions...")
- ❌ Make families feel guilty for being overwhelmed
- ❌ Ignore cultural dynamics (Moroccan family expectations)
- ❌ Be salesy or pushy
- ❌ Minimize their struggle ("It's not that hard to...")
- ❌ Use generic phrases ("quality care," "dedicated team")
- ❌ Write long paragraphs that exhausted people won't read

## Cultural Sensitivity Notes
- Respect Moroccan values of family duty and honor
- Acknowledge that asking for help can feel like failure
- Frame professional care as *enhancing* family care, not replacing it
- Be sensitive to multi-generational households
- Understand religious considerations (prayer times, gender preferences for nurses)
- Recognize economic pressures on Moroccan families

## Conversion Elements
- Multiple CTAs throughout (but never pushy)
- Emphasis on "free consultation" and "no commitment"
- 24/7 contact options (families research at night when loved ones sleep)
- WhatsApp as primary contact (most used in Morocco)
- Internal link to services page
- Subtle urgency ("You don't have to wait until you break")
